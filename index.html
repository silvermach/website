What Is SilverMach?

Build, breathe and bleed speed.

SilverMach is a competitive STEM Racing team competing at the STEM Racing Open Nationals 2026 — a national-level stage of the world's largest student engineering competition, endorsed by Formula 1. Beyond designing and racing a miniature F1 car, we operate across engineering, manufacturing, enterprise, branding, marketing, sponsorship acquisition, stakeholder engagement, and project management, proving that success in engineering comes from combining innovation, collaboration, communication, and leadership.

Our mission is to innovate, design, and optimise high performance engineering solutions through technical excellence, data driven development, and teamwork, demonstrating what young engineers can achieve through STEM Racing.

Technical excellence and continuous improvement drive everything from our CAD models to our race-day execution.

Engineering Analysis Suite

Simulate the risk. Engineer with data.

Two tools straight from our design process: a Monte Carlo race-time simulator that models real-world uncertainty in every input, and a decision-lineage tracker that documents why each design iteration evolved and was selected.

Simulation Inputs
Every parameter is drawn independently for each trial by a Box–Muller transform. Mass, drag, peak thrust, reaction time and μ use a truncated normal — a draw that falls below its physical bound is rejected and redrawn rather than clamped — while the four toe angles use a plain signed normal about 0°, so toe-in and toe-out are equally likely. Hover any field for what it means and how it reaches the solver.
Model: an 8 g CO₂ canister delivers a time-dependent thrust F(t) = F₀e−t/τ (choked blowdown), acting along the car's axis via cos θ. Four independent wheel toe angles add a scrub retard, and quadratic aerodynamic drag opposes motion throughout. τ, θ and the exhaust velocity behind F₀ are engineering assumptions, not measurements — the team holds no test-stand data. Tick “Show thrust curve” to see the exact F(t) the simulation integrates. All computation runs client-side in your browser.

Thrust Decay Curve — F(t) used by this simulation

— —
Plotted from the same SM.Thrust module the solver integrates — there is no second copy of the equation, so this curve cannot disagree with the physics above. τ and the exhaust velocity that sets F₀ are engineering assumptions; no measured thrust data exists. The solver integrates ΣF = F₀·cos θ·e−t/τ − ½ρCdAv² − Σᵢ μNi|sin δi| with RK4, so the only two resistance mechanisms modelled are aerodynamic drag and per-wheel scrub. Rolling resistance, axle/bearing friction and wheel rotational inertia are not implemented — the project holds no measurement for any of them, so none has been assumed.
